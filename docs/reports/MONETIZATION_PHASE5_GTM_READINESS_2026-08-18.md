## ICP and qualification rule

An account is qualified only when every hard criterion is evidenced:

1. It serves people relocating to or starting life/work in the Netherlands, or owns onboarding for international hires.
2. A current public source establishes the Netherlands-relevant service or employee population.
3. A public company contact route or role-level buying-team path exists; no personal email guessing.
4. The likely use case fits a 90-day operational pilot, not audience advertising.
5. No professional-service placement is proposed unless responsibility, disclosure and editorial separation can be stated truthfully.
6. The account is not represented as a customer, partner or active opportunity without direct evidence.

Suggested first buyer roles: International Mobility, People Operations, Employee Experience or relocation operations. These are hypotheses until an interview confirms problem ownership and budget authority.

## Five problem interviews

### Interview sample

1. Relocation operations lead at a first-wave provider.
2. International Mobility or People Operations lead at a Netherlands employer.
3. Employee Experience or onboarding owner at a second employer.
4. Language/inburgering-provider operations lead.
5. One professional-service provider with transparent responsibility, or a municipal/education stakeholder used only for trust/distribution learning.

### Neutral 25-minute guide

Ask for a recent real case before discussing YouNew:

1. “Tell me about the last international newcomer you helped in the Netherlands. Where did the process become unclear after arrival or employment start?”
2. “Which questions repeated, who answered them, and what did that consume in time or handoffs?”
3. “What sources or tools did the team use? Where did they become outdated, inconsistent or hard to localize?”
4. “What happens when guidance is wrong or incomplete? Which topics must always escalate to an official or qualified professional?”
5. “Who owns this problem, who approves a new supplier, and what evidence would be needed for a 90-day test?”
6. “How would you measure a useful pilot without claiming causation or exposing employee-level behavior?”
7. “What would make you reject or stop this pilot?”

Do not lead with price. At the end, ask one permission-based CTA: **“May I send a one-page pilot overview or open a short demo link for your review?”** Record consent and the chosen format.

### Interview evidence record

For each interview store: account, public role route, date, interviewer, consent status, problem verbatim, current workaround, frequency, consequence, owner, buying process, desired proof, objection, next step, time spent and direct cost. Separate verbatim statements from interpretation; do not store unnecessary personal data.

## Three demo variants

### A. Relocation-provider demo

- **Scenario:** an assignee reaches the Netherlands and needs municipality registration, DigiD, health-insurance and first-week navigation.
- **Show:** a co-branded but editorially independent landing route; location/language fallback; source names and verified dates; explicit escalation; clearly labelled sample aggregate report.
- **Value hypothesis:** fewer repetitive navigation handoffs and a maintained post-arrival layer.
- **Do not claim:** client status, response-time savings, adoption, reach or ROI.
- **CTA:** permission to scope one destination, one assignee cohort and 10–20 approved routes for 90 days.

### B. Employer international-hire demo

- **Scenario:** People Operations sends one maintained Netherlands onboarding link before start date.
- **Show:** newcomer checklist, source click, saved item after consent, language/city fallback and privacy-minimised sample reporting.
- **Value hypothesis:** a consistent handoff from HR policy to official practical steps without replacing HR, legal or tax advice.
- **Do not claim:** employee productivity, retention or reduced ticket volume without a measured baseline.
- **CTA:** permission to define the cohort, internal owner, privacy boundary and success measures for a paid trial.

### C. Language / professional-provider demo

- **Scenario:** a user reaches a relevant service only after official eligibility and escalation context is shown.
- **Show:** editorial result first; a separately labelled partner page; no sponsor input to emergency, health, legal, government or retrieval ranking; responsibility/disclosure fields.
- **Value hypothesis:** higher-quality, context-aware introductions without disguising commercial placement as advice.
- **Do not claim:** endorsement, guaranteed eligibility, professional outcome or paid ranking.
- **CTA:** permission to review a one-page founding-partner boundary and disclosure model.

### Lost / rejection reason taxonomy

Use one primary code plus optional factual notes:

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `no_observed_problem` | The account does not recognise a material onboarding/navigation problem |
| `existing_solution_sufficient` | Current internal or vendor workflow is adequate |
| `wrong_owner` | Contact cannot own or route the decision |
| `no_budget` | Problem exists but no budget is available in the test window |
| `timing` | Valid need, wrong procurement or operational timing |
| `insufficient_proof` | Account requires evidence YouNew does not yet have |
| `security_privacy` | Data, security or privacy posture is unacceptable or incomplete |
| `content_trust` | Coverage, review or professional-responsibility boundary is insufficient |
| `price_value` | Price is not justified by the accepted value hypothesis |
| `scope_delivery` | Pilot scope, SLA or capacity is unsuitable |
| `no_response_after_sequence` | Approved finite sequence completed without response |
| `other_explicit` | A different explicit reason is captured factually |

Do not infer a rejection reason from silence before the approved sequence ends.
